Periscope reads that shadow with an ordinary camera, turns it into the person's bearing every frame, and tells a forklift or robot to stop before they step into view.

Watching Signal is readable.

      Scored against ground truth

      The floor beside a corner is a one-pixel camera pointed at the hidden hallway.

      Each spot on the floor next to the wall edge sees the hidden side through a slit that widens with its angle from the wall. A spot at 30° gathers light from the first 30° of the hidden hallway; a spot at 60° from the first 60°. Comparing neighbouring angles recovers how much light comes from each direction, and a person shows up as a moving dip in their own colour.

      This is MIT's CornerCam (Bouman et al., ICCV 2017). Periscope adds what a product needs: a calibrated wedge, shake and lighting compensation, a noise model that knows when it can't see, and an agent that acts on it.

      1. CalibrateFive tape marks fix the corner, the wall line and a true floor angle for every pixel, or the corner is found automatically.
      2. LearnTwo seconds of empty hallway set the background and the noise floor.
      3. Reconstruct90 angular bins, divided by the background, inverted with a regularised least squares into 45 hidden directions.
      4. Track & actA noise-calibrated score finds the person, an α-β filter follows the bearing, the agent sends stop.

      Bearing, not distance

      One corner gives the angle only. Distance needs a second edge (a doorway) or a second camera.

      Needs light and a steady view

      Range falls roughly as 1/d³. A dim hallway, grey clothing or a shaking mount shrink it; the agent says so instead of guessing.

      Compression costs range

      A normal phone video at ~13 Mbps loses about half the range. Record near-lossless, or bin the floor on the device and send 1 KB per frame.
